CAT 2021–2025 DILR: Topic-Wise Question Breakdown, Difficulty
Calibration & Knowledge Brief
Across the 15 exam slots spanning CAT 2021 through CAT 2025, the Data Interpretation and
Logical Reasoning (DILR) section comprises 66 distinct sets totaling 312 questions . While
every slot tests the same core analytical muscles—extracting structure from dense constraints,
translating visual data into arithmetic, and managing uncertainty—the section underwent a
structural shift in 2024. From 2021 to 2023, each slot contained 20 questions across 4 sets
(typically two 4-question sets and two 6-question sets in 2021, or four 5-question sets in 2022–
2023). Starting in 2024 and continuing through 2025, the section expanded to 22 questions
across 5 sets per slot (typically three 4-question sets and two 5-question sets).
Because the time limit remained 40 minutes throughout all five years, the 2024–2025 format
rewards rapid set triage even more heavily than before. Solving 2 to 3 well-chosen sets
accurately in 40 minutes consistently outperforms attempting 4 sets partially. This guide
organizes all 66 sets into six practitioner-focused topic categories, calibrates each set's difficulty
and expected solve time to a 40-minute exam clock, and provides the exact mathematical and
logical toolkit required to master each category.
1. The 5-Year DILR Landscape & 40-Minute Calibration
To use past papers effectively, you must understand both what appears most frequently and
how to budget your 40 minutes. Pure calculation-heavy DI has steadily given way to hybrid
sets —visual charts layered with logical conditions (such as the CAT 2024 Slot 1 Bar Chart on
countries visited or the CAT 2025 Slot 2 Sustainability Index scatter plot) and Quant-Based LR
puzzles built around averages, medians, and integer constraints.

Prerequisite Knowledge & Solving Framework
Solving Visual DI sets quickly requires fluency in chart-reading conventions and mental
approximation so you never get bogged down in exact decimal division when options are well￾spaced.
● Reading Non-Standard Charts:
● Candlestick Plots (2024 Slot 1): Green (bullish) means $\text{Close} > \text{Open}$ , so
the bottom of the box is Open and the top is Close; Red (bearish) means $\text{Close} <
\text{Open}$ , so the top of the box is Open and the bottom is Close. The upper and
lower wicks mark the day's High and Low.
● Gantt / Schedule Bars (2021 Slot 2 & Slot 3): Processing time or duration equals $
\text{Right Endpoint} - \text{Left Endpoint}$ . Check whether endpoints are inclusive or
date-differences (e.g., Sep 1 to Sep 5 is 4 days, as explicitly defined in the 2021 Slot 2
example).
● Bubble & Scatter Plots (2022 Slot 2, 2024 Slot 2, 2025 Slot 2): When bubble area is
proportional to a variable (PRD in 2024 Slot 2), compare areas visually or via radius
squared ( $A \propto r^2$ ), never by radius alone. When axes show percentage
changes rather than absolute values (2025 Slot 2), convert percentages into multiplier
fractions (e.g., $+25\% = \times \frac{5}{4}$ , $-20\% = \times \frac{4}{5}$ ) and use the
integer constraint of the underlying index to lock in exact values.
● Step-Line Charts (2025 Slot 3): A vertical jump at time $t$ marks the completion of an
item; the horizontal plateau length before the jump equals the time taken for that item.
Comparing "All Puzzles" against "Visual Puzzles" reveals which jumps belong to
"Number-based Puzzles" by subtraction.
● Core Arithmetic Shortcuts:
● Percentage Growth & Compounding: For small annual rates $r\%$ over $n$ years
(2024 Slot 3), $(1 + r)^n \approx 1 + nr$ is accurate enough to rank countries, and $
\text{GDP per capita}$ falls if and only if $\text{GDP growth rate} < \text{Population
growth rate}$ .
● Attrition / Stock-Flow Identity (2022 Slot 2): $\text{Employees Lost in Year } t =
\text{Start}_t + \text{New Hires}t - \text{Start}{t+1}$ . Always write this identity down
before reading the questions.
● Common CAT Traps:
● Relative Bases: In 2024 Slot 3 (Countries 1–8), GDP is given as a fraction of Country
9's GDP, while GDP per capita is a fraction of Country 10's. Population is proportional to
$\frac{\text{GDP fraction}}{\text{GDP per capita fraction}}$ —the unknown constants for
Country 9 and Country 10 cancel out in any cross-country comparison.
● Axis Reading: Always check whether the axis starts at zero and whether gridlines
represent 1-unit, 2-unit, or 10% increments before estimating values.

Prerequisite Knowledge & Solving Framework
To crack Quant-Based LR sets within 10–12 minutes, you need four algebraic and statistical
tools at your fingertips:
● Reconstructing Data from Summary Statistics:
● Mean $\leftrightarrow$ Sum: Always convert means into totals immediately ( $
\text{Sum} = \text{Mean} \times N$ ). In 2023 Slot 1, a mean of 3.4 across 5 ratings
immediately gives a row sum of 17.
● 2-Day Averages (2023 Slot 2): If the 2-day average on Day $k$ is $A_k$ , then
$S_{k-1} + S_k = 2 A_k$ . Subtracting consecutive equations yields $S_{k+1} - S_{k-1}
= 2(A_{k+1} - A_k)$ , letting you chain all daily totals from a single anchor value.

--- PAGE ---

● Median, Mode & Range: For 5 sorted integers $x_1 \le x_2 \le x_3 \le x_4 \le x_5$ , the
median is $x_3$ and the range is $x_5 - x_1$ . If a mode is unique, it must appear at
least twice, and any other value must appear strictly fewer times.
● Prime Factorization & Divisibility Constraints:
● In 2022 Slot 1, dividing each funding amount by 1,000 and factorizing into primes ( $2, 3,
5, 7, 11, 13$ ) instantly reveals which interviewer awarded a token to which candidate—
turning a 12-minute puzzle into an 8-minute one.
● In 2024 Slot 3 (Food Grains), using the fact that missing protein/fat/other values are
multiples of 4 while carbs are multiples of 5 narrows each row sum ( $C + P + F + O =
100$ ) to at most one or two valid tuples.
● Flow Conservation & Weighted Averages:
● For closed-group interactions (2025 Slot 3 Call Minutes, 2025 Slot 3 Trade), the total
outgoing minutes from Xitel users to Yocel users must equal the total incoming minutes
at Yocel users from Xitel users. Build a $6 \times 6$ directed matrix and fill in the zeros
first.
● For weighted averages ( $W = w_1 x_1 + (1 - w_1) x_2$ ), look for two entities with the
same score in one component so their difference in $W$ isolates the weight $w_1$
directly.
● Common CAT Traps:
● Forgetting that "distinct integers" or "scale of 1 to 5" bounds the maximum/minimum
possible values of a sum, often forcing an extreme configuration (e.g., all remaining
items must be 1 or 5).

constraining clue ("anchor clue") to unlock the matrix.

Prerequisite Knowledge & Solving Framework
● The Anchor-First Rule: Never process clues in the order they are written. Scan all
clues first for universal exclusions ("never", "none", "only"), exact counts (totals per
row/column), and extremes (highest/lowest). In 2024 Slot 2 (Numbers 1–10), 10 must
be in Row 1 (since columns decrease top-to-bottom) and 1 must be at the bottom of its
column or left of its row—placing 10 and 1 immediately collapses the grid.
● Sub-Grid Partitioning: When a set has 3+ dimensions (e.g., Author $\times$ Country
$\times$ Area $\times$ Issue in 2021 Slot 1), separate the static attributes (Country,
Area, total papers) into a master profile table first, and only then map them onto the
schedule grid (Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct).
● Envy-Free & Threshold Logic: In 2021 Slot 2 (Bundles), if a person does not envy
anyone, their own bundle value must be $\ge$ their valuation of every other person's
bundle. Rank each person's top object values first—an envy-free allocation almost
always forces each person to receive at least one of their top 2–3 valued items.
● Common CAT Traps:
● Partial Determinacy: CAT loves asking "For how many slots can placement NOT be
determined with certainty?" When you hit a symmetric pair (two cells or two people that
can swap without violating any rule), do not waste time looking for a missing clue—
document the swap explicitly as $(X/Y)$ and move to the questions.

Prerequisite Knowledge & Solving Framework
● State-Transition Tables: Draw a time-indexed table (columns = rounds/quarters/
time-slots, rows = players/counters/segments). Never track state changes in your head
or in margins.
● Fleet Size & Queue Math (2022 Slot 1, 2023 Slot 1, 2023 Slot 2):
● Minimum Trains / Fleet Formula: A train leaving Terminal A at 6:00 am takes
$T_{\text{trip}}$ minutes to reach B and rests $R$ minutes before returning. The
minimum trains needed on a line equals the number of departures from both terminals
combined during one full round-trip cycle ( $2(T_{\text{trip}} + R)$ ), or equivalently,
tracking how many trains depart before the first 6:00 am train is ready for its return trip at
B.
● Counter Queues: Track counter free-times rather than applicant arrival times. If 2
Schengen counters each take $t_S$ minutes, an applicant arriving at 9:20 am enters
the queue and starts only when max(arrival_time, next_counter_free_time) aligns with a
valid gate/slot.
● Segment-Flow Accounting (2025 Slot 1 Train): A ticket from $i$ to $j$ occupies
every segment between $i$ and $j$ . Therefore, $\text{Occupancy}(k \to k+1) =
\text{Occupancy}(k-1 \to k) + \text{Boardings at } k - \text{Alightings at } k$ . This single
conservation equation solves all 4-segment train/bus problems.
● Common CAT Traps:
● Tie-Break Direction: In dynamic ranking sets (2021 Slot 3 Javelin, 2025 Slot 1
InnovateX), tie-break rules are reversed or nuanced (e.g., "prevailing higher rank retains
rank" vs. "higher rating in the latest quarter ranks higher"). Underline the exact tie-break
clause before simulating Round 1.

Prerequisite Knowledge & Solving Framework
● Graph & Eulerian/Hamiltonian Intuition (2024 Slot 2 Walkways):
● When a problem asks for the maximum distance without repeating any point
(vertex) , you are looking for a longest simple path/cycle—check degree-2 vertices and
bottlenecks first, and calculate diagonal lengths using the Pythagorean theorem from
the given rectangular dimensions.
● When a problem allows repeating points but not paths (edges) , count the degree
(number of connected edges) at each node. By Euler's theorem, any node with an odd
degree (other than start and end) forces at least one incident edge to be left unused.
● Manhattan Distance on Grids (2024 Slot 3 ATMs): On a rectangular road grid, the
road distance between $(R_i, V_j)$ and $(R_m, V_n)$ is simply $|R_i - R_m| + |V_j -
V_n|$ . Use the exact distance clue (e.g., 12 km) to lock in which intersections are
occupied before solving the row/column cash sums.
● Modular Arithmetic for Circular Movement (2025 Slot 1 & Slot 3): Label 7 seats as
$0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 \pmod 7$ . "Immediately left/right" is $\pm 1$ , and "second to left/
right" is $\pm 2$ . Summing the net displacement across known rounds quickly pins
down missing pass types.
● Common CAT Traps:
● Directionality & Entry/Exit Constraints: In 2024 Slot 2, visitors must enter and exit at C
and cannot pass residences on AH and ML—meaning you must delete those vertices/
edges from the graph before finding the longest walk.

Prerequisite Knowledge & Solving Framework
● 3-Attribute Venn / $2 \times 2 \times 2$ Tree Method (2022 Slot 1, Slot 2, Slot 3):
● Whenever you see three binary attributes (e.g., Domestic/Foreign, Cosmetic/
